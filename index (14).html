About

Teaching the students other systems overlook.

I serve ninth through twelfth graders in 90-minute EL blocks at Laurel High School, across Emerging (ELPA21 Levels 1–2) and Progressing (Levels 3–4) tracks. Every lesson runs on a four-lane differentiation model so a newcomer and a near-exit student can sit in the same room and both leave with something they didn't have before.

Outside the classroom I write, code and publish under EL Publishing: a full 36-week curriculum, interactive practice suites, bilingual standards guides, and a book manuscript, Making Them Count: Teaching English Learners in Mississippi's Overlooked Districts. I'm bilingual in English and Spanish and pursuing National Board certification in ENL, with a long view toward EL district coordination.

Sample lesson · anatomy of a Storm Studio block

One text. Four lanes. Five ways to prove it.

Open the live lesson and work it the way a student would, or read the breakdown first. This is the model every Storm Studio block runs on: one culturally familiar text, a 90-minute arc, and every student leaving with a product.

Reading Check: How Tamales Are Made

An informational feature on the Mesoamerican tamal, from masa to steam, followed by five activities that move from recognition to independent production. The text is the same for every lane; the scaffold, the language demand and the output change.

Grades 9–12Informational · process textELPA21 L1–490-min blockEN with ES glosses
Open the live lesson →
Targets
ELP 1 · construct meaning from grade-level text
ELP 3 · speak and write about texts
ELP 9 · create clear, coherent speech and text
RI.9-10.3 · analyze how a sequence of steps unfolds
Language function
Sequencing and explaining a process: first, then, after, while, until, finally
Output
Ordered sequence · illustrated guide · oral explanation (recorded) · progress report
Evidence
Downloadable student progress report; recording for speaking portfolio
  1. 0–10Launch

    Photo of a tamal, one question: Who makes these in your family? Oral activation in any language, then three target words on the board: masa, husk, steam.

  2. 10–30Read the feature

    Shared read with glossed vocabulary and step images. Newcomers track the images; Progressing readers annotate signal words.

  3. 30–45Build a Tamal

    Sequencing: put the steps in order. The lesson checks the order instantly, so the teacher's time goes to the students who need it.

  4. 45–60Evidence Detective & Kitchen Rescue

    Find the sentence that proves it; then solve a problem (the masa is too dry) using the text. Reading becomes reasoning.

  5. 60–80My Tamale Guide

    Students write and illustrate their own how-to. Frames for Lanes 1–2; open prompt with a transition-word bank for Lanes 3–4.

  6. 80–90From Your Kitchen to Mine

    Record a 45-second oral explanation of a dish from home. Export the progress report. Done.

LANE 1 · NEWCOMER

Point, order, name

Image-only sequencing, vocabulary matching with pictures, a guide built from sentence frames: First, you ___. Then, you ___. Oral task may be answered in L1 with two English words.

LANE 2 · EMERGING

Frame and extend

Text sequencing with the article open. Guide uses frames plus one original sentence per step. Oral task in short English sentences with a word bank.

LANE 3 · PROGRESSING

Explain and justify

Evidence Detective required. Guide written in paragraphs using the transition bank. Oral task includes one reason (because, so that).

LANE 4 · CHALLENGE

Compare and critique

Kitchen Rescue with a second problem of their own. Guide compares their dish to the tamal. Oral task argued, not just described.

Familiar content, new language.

When the subject is already known, working memory is free for English. Nearly every student in the room has eaten a tamal.

Same text, different demand.

Differentiation lives in the task, not in a watered-down text. Every lane reads the real article.

Four skills, one block.

Reading, writing, listening and speaking all happen before the bell, in the shape ELPA21 will test them.

Evidence walks out the door.

The progress report and the recording are the artifacts: for the student folder, for §15 progress monitoring, for the NBCT portfolio.
